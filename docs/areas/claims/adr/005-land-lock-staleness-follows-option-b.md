# ADR-claims-005 — Only a waiting acquirer takes over an idle land lock, after 45 minutes

A holder that dies mid-landing leaves its lock at a head that never merges. On issue 629 the owner chose option B: a one-shot `status` or `acquire` takes over only the lock of a merged or closed pull request, and only `acquire --wait` takes over a held lock, once it has itself watched the lock unchanged for 45 minutes on its own monotonic clock, with nothing persisted. A holder whose lock stays at one sha through a long wait keeps that watch from running out by moving a second ref, the beat `refs/agent-locks/land-beat/<branch>`, and a waiter restarts its watch when the lock or the beat changes (issue 971). The rejected alternatives were judging idleness from event timestamps, which needs a wall clock and a persisted record, an unattended one-shot takeover, which could merge two branches at once, and a holder releasing and retaking its lock to show it is alive, which hands the lock to any waiter in between. The cost is that a dead holder blocks one-shot acquirers until a waiter's 45 minutes pass, and that a holder which keeps one sha for 45 minutes without refreshing loses its lock. A green lease (issue 1679) is also taken over by the first waiter in line when its beats stop for 3 minutes or it has held 40 minutes (issue 1904 raised it from 20: the slowest required check of 26 Nov took 30 minutes, and a lander whose own check outlasted the bound was taken over before it could merge), by the forge's clock; a release and a batch train take no lease and keep the 45-minute takeover. The cost is that a late lease can cut a hold short. A step of a landing that runs its own long commands under the lease starts `hold --detach`, which beats once and leaves a background process beating every 60 seconds for up to 50 minutes, so the 3-minute rule is not lost to a step longer than 3 minutes.
