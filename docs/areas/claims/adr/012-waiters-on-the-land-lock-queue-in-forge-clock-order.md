# ADR-claims-012 — Waiters on the land lock queue in forge-clock order

- **Status:** accepted
- **Decided in:** issue 1001, amended by issue 1018; extends ADR-claims-004 and ADR-claims-005

## Context

`forge-claim land acquire --wait` polled every 120 seconds and nothing ordered the waiters. A released lock went to whichever waiter polled first. One wait for a single pull request ran 49 minutes and lost two free windows of 43 and 50 seconds to landers that had arrived later, and the lock waits of one four-issue batch added up to 101.7 minutes.

Only ref operations and reads are available across hosts, and the hosts' clocks cannot be trusted against each other (ADR-claims-003).

## Decision

The queue decides only who tries. The lock is still granted by nothing but the create and the compare-and-swap of ADR-claims-004, so no queue state, true, false or forged, can make two holders.

A waiter registers as a ref `refs/agent-locks/land-wait/<branch>/<since>-<pr>-<beat>` at its waiting head. `<since>` and `<beat>` are seconds of the forge's clock, the `Date` header of the free `rate_limit` call, read once in every poll: `<since>` when the waiter first asked, `<beat>` at its last refresh. The name carries everything, so one listing (`GET git/matching-refs`, which the forge answers whole, unpaginated) gives the whole queue and no commit object is written. A refresh creates the ref with the new beat and then deletes the old one.

- **Order.** A pull request's place is the smallest `<since>` of its live registrations, ties going to the smaller number. The clock is read before the registration is written, so two waiters that arrive within the seconds between one's clock read and its registration being listed can both find themselves first; the create then decides, as it did before. No host clock is compared.
- **Liveness.** A registration whose beat is more than 390 seconds (three polls and a margin) behind the forge's clock, or more than 60 seconds ahead of it, is dead: skipped, and deleted by whoever lists it. A waiter ahead whose pull request is not open is skipped and deleted too; only the oldest one ahead is read, so the check costs one call a poll.
- **Who tries.** A waiter with a live open pull request ahead of it creates no free lock and takes no closed one over. Nothing else asks the queue. `already_held` and `moved` concern only the caller's own lock and are answered before the queue is read, so a holder that runs `--wait` again is never held behind it and never fails with it. The idle takeover does not depend on the caller's place: its own 45-minute watch (ADR-claims-005) is the bound, as before. The second attempt of a following release, right after it deleted its own lock at the head it started from, is not queued either: the lock it gave up would otherwise lie free behind a registered lander, which could land while the release holds (ADR-claims-009).
- **The grace.** A waiter that has itself watched the lock free for 60 seconds, or has itself seen it closed for 150 seconds, tries whatever its place. A live first waiter takes a free lock within 20 seconds and a closed one within one poll of 120 seconds: a merge leaves the lock's oid unchanged, so the 20-second read of the ref does not see it and only a whole poll does. Each grace is longer than that, so it costs the order nothing while the first in line works, and it bounds what a hung or dead first waiter costs to a minute before a free lock and two and a half before a closed one, instead of 390 seconds.
- **A failing queue never fails a wait.** Any failed read or write of the queue (the clock, the listing, the pull request ahead, the registration's create or delete, whatever the error) is no failed poll: that poll attempts unordered, as a client before this decision does, at the 120-second rate, and answers `position` `null`.
- **Poll rate.** While nobody is ahead of it, a waiter reads the lock ref alone every 20 seconds between two polls and polls at once when it changed or is free. Free alone is enough, and one failed poll does not stop these reads (issue 1018). The first in line once left a lock free for 87 seconds while it waited, and the waiter behind took it when its 60-second grace ended; the grace did what it is for. Two paths in the code lead there, and the transcripts, which record no poll of a waiter, cannot tell them apart: a whole poll that finds the lock closed and loses its takeover to the holder's release ends having read the lock free, so a read that only compared saw no change for the rest of the 120 seconds; and a poll that failed once (a transport error, or a swap refused on a lagging read) ended the 20-second reads until the next whole poll. After a failed poll a read that fails starts no poll, so failed polls keep their 120-second rate. The issue asked for the faster poll when the holder's pull-request state suggests a release is near; reading only the lock ref is cheaper than judging that state and sees every release, so the rate depends on the place alone.
- **A wait that runs into its bound keeps its place.** The issue asked that the registration be removed on `land-contended`. The delivery programs ask again at once after that answer, and a removed registration would send a pull request that waited 50 minutes to the back of the line. The registration is left instead, and the next `--wait` of that pull request adopts the `<since>` of a registration still live: the re-call must come within the 390 seconds of liveness to keep the place, and the delivery programs re-call within seconds. One nobody comes back to dies after 390 seconds, passed sooner by the grace above. Every other exit deletes the registration: the lock taken, `no_open_pr`, an error. While it waits a process deletes only registrations it created itself, so two waits of one pull request share a place and do not undo each other.
- **A wait that takes the lock in its first poll never registers.** It registers once an attempt did not give it the lock.
- **Not ordered:** a one-shot `acquire` and a client that predates this decision. They register nothing and take a free lock when they find one. The holders' rechecks before a merge are one-shot calls and are unchanged, at two forge calls.

`acquire --wait` answers two more members on success and on `land-contended`: `position` (1 is first in line; `null` when the queue was never read, as for a caller that already holds the lock, or when its last read failed) and `ahead` (the pull requests ahead, oldest first), as last read.

## Consequences

- Forge calls of one waiter behind a held lock and behind another waiter: 9 per 120-second poll (was 5), of which 2 are writes of its registration and none is a write of the lock (was 1 refused create): 4.5 calls and 1 write a minute. The first in line reads no pull request ahead (8 per poll) and adds 5 reads of the lock ref between two polls: 6.5 calls a minute, about 390 an hour, with the same 1 write a minute. One call of every poll is the `rate_limit` read, which the forge does not count. A waiter no longer sends a create for a lock it read as held.
- Mixed fleet. Among registered waiters the order is first come, first served, within the tolerance above. An old client or a one-shot `acquire` is outside the order: it is never held back by the queue and cannot deadlock it, because registered waiters wait only for the lock, for a live registered waiter ahead, and never more than a minute before a free lock. A registered waiter can still lose a free window to an old client; only the first in line competes with them, with a poll six times as fast, and the waiters behind it do not compete until they are first. Strict order therefore holds only once every waiting host runs the new `forge-claim`.
- A dead first waiter costs the others at most a minute before a free lock and at most 150 seconds before a closed one (the lock of a merged or closed pull request), each counted from when the waiter itself first saw the lock so. The idle takeover is unchanged: every waiter makes it at the end of its own watch, whatever its place.
- A lock that is released and retaken between two polls of a waiter that is not first looks continuously free to it; after the grace it tries, which can cost a place, never the lock.
- A host whose queue reads fail waits unordered and so can pass registered waiters; that is the behaviour before this decision, and it is preferred to a wait that ends on a failure of something that grants nothing.
- Anyone who can write refs can forge a registration and so delay registered waiters by up to a minute per free window; the same party could already move or delete the lock.
