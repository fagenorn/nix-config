# ADR-console-038 — A manager's question shows its pictures on the card and full size in a viewer

- **Status:** accepted; amended by ADR-console-041 and ADR-console-043
- **Decided in:** #1090

## Context

A manager that asks the owner a question about something it built or checked has a screenshot that would settle it, and the owner reads the question on the page with no way to see it. ADR-console-036 gave the console a store for a manager's pictures and a read that only the page can use; ADR-console-019 made each question of a round an inbox item. This decision joins them: what a question may carry, what the card shows, and how the owner looks at a picture full size. The brief and report cards of #1091 will show pictures the same way and reuse the viewer decided here.

## Decision

**D1. The round's pictures.** A question of a round may carry `pictures`, at most 8, each `{"picture": <id>, "line": <text>, "pair": "before" | "after"}` with `pair` optional. The `picture` is the id of a PNG this sprint stored (ADR-console-036), the `line` is one line of at most 140 characters that the owner reads beside the picture, and a question marks at most one picture `before` and at most one `after`; a second of either is refused. An id this sprint never stored is refused, and so is one another sprint stored, and so is a stored id whose file is gone or is not a regular file. That presence check reads the store and changes nothing; it runs under the ingest lock that every retention sweep also holds, so no sweep runs between the check and the append, and once the round is logged its `questions.asked` row carries the ids in a top-level `pictures` list, which keeps each file from every later sweep (ADR-console-036 D5). A round with no pictures logs exactly the row it logged before. A row read back from the log keeps every shape rule and the count of 8, and its line limit is the log's 500 characters rather than 140; the view cuts a line to 140.

**D2. The command.** `sprint ask` takes a `file` for each picture, checks the whole round locally first (count, shape, line, pair) and refuses a bad one with no request sent, then reads and size-checks every file and uploads each in order, then sends the round with each `file` replaced by the id the console returned. A refused upload stops before the round is sent.

**D3. The card.** A question card shows its pictures under its context: one thumbnail for each, its line under it, in a row that wraps. Every thumbnail fills its column's width and takes the picture's own shape, up to 180 pixels tall (240 at 480 pixels); only a picture taller than that is fitted whole on a light tint of the card, never cropped, so a tall phone screenshot and a wide banner both read whole, and a pair lines up at its top edge. A `before` and an `after` that are both present sit side by side, labelled Before and After; a lone one is a plain picture with no label. The rows of the other open questions show their pictures the same way. At 480 pixels the pair stacks, and every picture stays inside its card. Only the cards change; the Work column's markup, CSS and render code are untouched.

**D4. The viewer.** A click, or Enter or Space on a focused thumbnail, opens one shared modal viewer. It steps through the card's pictures one at a time in the card's order with the arrow keys and its Previous and Next buttons, which stop at the ends, and it shows `<i> of <n>`. Previous, `<i> of <n>`, Next and Close sit on one bar at the top that stays in view while the pictures, placed at the top just under it, scroll under it, and at 480 pixels each button is at least 44 pixels tall. The viewer covers the page with an opaque surface of the page's own colours, so nothing of the page shows through, and the page behind it does not scroll while it is open; closing it puts the page back at the scroll position it had when it opened. A picture of a pair shows both pictures side by side, labelled, with the current one marked. It opens with focus on the viewer itself, which draws no focus ring. An arrow key or a button moves the picture and leaves focus where it was; only a focused button the move disables at an end hands focus back to the viewer. Escape or Close closes it and returns focus to the thumbnail of the picture it showed, and it closes by itself when its card leaves the page. While it is open it owns the keyboard: the page's digit, Enter, `a`, `g` and Escape-undo keys do nothing. Every other child of the page's body is `inert`, so no control behind it can take focus or be activated, and Tab and Shift+Tab move only among the viewer's enabled buttons in the bar's order, wrapping at both ends, Tab from the viewer itself reaching the first and Shift+Tab the last; Enter and Space on a viewer button are left to the button. At 480 pixels the viewer's pair stacks and its pictures fit the width. The brief and report cards of #1091 reuse the same gallery and viewer.

**D5. Fetching.** The page fetches each picture once with `GET /api/picture/<id>` and its `X-Console-Token` header (ADR-console-036 D7), keeps the answer as an object URL, and gives every thumbnail and viewer image of that id the same URL; a picture drawn twice is fetched once, across every frame. An object URL is revoked once no picture drawn on the page uses it. A picture that cannot be fetched reads `The picture could not be loaded.` The page's content security policy admits these images with `img-src data: blob:`; no other directive changes, and no `src` is ever a console address.

## Consequences

- ADR-console-019's questions may carry pictures and ADR-console-036's pictures are shown to the owner; both are amended by this decision. ADR-console-002's rule that the page is one self-contained document holds: the viewer is markup in the page, and the policy gains only `blob:` for images.
- ADR-console-033's rules that a card fits the screen and reads plainly extend to the gallery at 480 pixels.
- A manager's contract names `pictures` in its round; a round that carries none is unchanged.
- The viewer's modal focus is the page's first use of `inert`; the page tests check the property and the key handling, and a browser check confirms native focus and activation.
- Related: ADR-console-002, ADR-console-019, ADR-console-033, ADR-console-036.
