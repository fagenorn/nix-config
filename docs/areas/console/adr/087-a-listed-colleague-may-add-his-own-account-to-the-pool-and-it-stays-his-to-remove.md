# ADR-console-087 — A listed colleague may add his own account to the pool, and it stays his to remove

- **Status:** accepted
- **Decided in:** #1342

## Context

ADR-console-066 D3 keeps accounts and sign-in owner-only, so a guest never holds an account of the pool, and ADR-console-023 makes the console alone the chooser of the profile a new session runs on. On 19 Nov the owner decided that a listed colleague may add his own Claude account to the pool, and answered five design questions. This record keeps those answers so the slices that build them have a decision to cite; it changes no code.

## Decision

**D1. Whose sprints run on a colleague's account.** His own sprints first; anyone's sprints when it has room. This amends ADR-console-023 D1: among the profiles with room, a colleague's own account is the choice for a sprint that colleague started, and any other sprint may use it only when it has room under the rule there. The owner's pin (ADR-console-023 D1) outranks this preference: a pinned profile with room is the choice for every sprint, a colleague's own included, and his own account is preferred only among the profiles left when no pin is in effect. This record adds no other precedence over ADR-console-023 D1.

**D2. Consent.** Before Sign in, the guest page shows one plain sentence saying the credential is kept on the owner's host under the owner's user, so any process of the owner can use it. Sign in stays disabled until he ticks that he agrees.

**D3. Who may sign it out or remove it.** The colleague, for his own account only, and the owner. The owner may also exclude it from the pool (ADR-console-023 D1); the colleague may not.

**D4. How the pages show whose it is.** The guest page labels the account `yours` for its owner and `Account n` for everyone else (ADR-console-086 D3). The owner's page shows the colleague's login beside the row.

**D5. Removal.** At once. This is an exception to ADR-console-026 (D6 and its Consequences), which lets a session under a removed profile keep running, and it covers only a colleague's account removed by its colleague or the owner: each manager running on that account restarts on the pool's choice (ADR-console-023 D1), and when no profile has room or none is signed in, that manager waits as ADR-console-023 D4 says, with the earliest reset or with no resume time. Any other session on the account keeps running, and removing the owner's own account is unchanged.

**D6. Spend stays the owner's.** A colleague's account changes nothing in ADR-console-086 D2: money is the owner's bill, and a spend per account or per project would reveal work on projects the colleague is not listed for. The guest view's `spend` stays null, whoever holds the account.

## Considered options

- Only the colleague's own sprints on his account: leaves room unused while the owner's sprints wait.
- Any sprint first on a colleague's account: spends a colleague's allowance before his own work needs it.
- No consent sentence: the colleague would not learn that any process of the owner can use the credential.
- Showing the colleague his account's spend: the same figure per account would reveal work on projects he is not listed for.

## Consequences

- Amends ADR-console-066 D3 (a guest may add and remove his own account) and ADR-console-023 D1 (whose sprints a profile serves).
- The account and its credential are still the owner's host's. The trust boundary is the one ADR-console-077 D3 restates for the shared Mac, which replaced ADR-console-066 D1's loopback argument: the owner's processes can use the credential and other local users cannot reach the console, and the consent in D2 states that to the colleague.
- Built in #1343: the console chooses the `colleague-<n>` name itself, and the `account.added` row carries the colleague's login as `by`, which ends on a completed removal of the profile and is retained past the usual retention. Three guest account writes, `POST /api/guest-account`, `/api/guest-signin-code` and `/api/guest-signin-cancel`, each act on his own profile only. The add carries consent as `true` on the wire (`{"consent": true}`, with the shown `attempt` when the page asks again for a link it lacks: the console then only hands out that live sign-in's link, creates nothing and refuses an attempt that is over) and the console picks the profile: his own live sign-in, else one of his profiles that is not signed in, else a new one. The console reads the profile listing before it chooses the name (an unreadable listing creates and records nothing), and the ownership row is appended in the block that stores the listing read after the launcher returned, with no access check between, so a revocation during the add leaves the profile his and never one that is no one's. A profile is his only when this console saw the launcher return for a name it chose after a listing that did not hold it, and only while its directory is the one that add recorded (the row carries the directory's identity: its inode, its creation time where the platform gives one, which Linux does not, and a random marker file the console wrote into the directory as soon as the launcher returned; the replacement the launcher makes holds no marker, so a freed inode that the filesystem gives the replacement at once, as ext4 does, cannot make it his): a launcher that fails leaves its name reserved and unowned, so a profile of that name that appears later (the owner's, made with the CLI) is never his, and his retry takes a new name; a launcher that returned but whose listing could not be read keeps its name and the directory's identity for his retry, which takes the profile only if its directory is still that one, else takes a new name (only where another profile is listed to show the profile root: with no other profile listed a listing that fails after the launcher returned leaves the profile unowned and his retry makes another, which the owner may remove); and a profile the owner removed with the CLI and made again under the name is no longer his, in his sign-in, code and cancel and in the labels. The window between the launcher's return and the marker is a stated limit: an owner who replaces the directory in it takes the name, and where no other profile is listed to show the profile root the marker is written after the second listing instead. An add whose exclusive marker could not be written is refused and the profile stays unowned, and a new profile's sign-in status is read before its row: it must read signed out, since a name the owner removed with the CLI can still find the launcher's keychain entry of the earlier profile, which must not become his (a status that is signed in or cannot be read refuses the add, the profile staying unowned). The link a colleague waits for is handed out only while the profile is still his. A page reloaded during a sign-in sends the shown attempt with its press, so a run that ended in another tab is refused, never turned into another account. A copy of a marked directory with its marker is a stated limit too. The reservation lives in the console's memory: a restart between the launcher's creation and the row leaves an unowned profile the owner may remove. The code and the cancel carry the attempt the add answered and act on that attempt only, so a stale tab cannot stop a newer sign-in. The answer carries the attempt, the link and the route, without `port` and `forward`, and with no link for a loopback-only run. The link a colleague is handed is the client's authorization request on one of its own endpoints with its manual return address and nothing else of a printed URL. The guest page carries the guest write token in its HTML (the page's authentication contract, ADR-console-008 and the guest term in `CONTEXT.md`, which main already held before this slice) and in no other guest response; "no token" of the issue's third criterion is read as the owner's token, a credential and the sign-in client's output in every guest response, and the guest token outside the page. Whether the owner meant the first page too to carry none is not settled by this record.
- Built in #1361: signing a colleague's account in is his alone, so the owner's inbox raises no sign-in card for a profile with an owning login, the owner's own start of its sign-in is refused in plain words and starts no run, and the capacity strip says the signed-out account waits for that colleague.
- Built in #1344: `manager.session_profile` and `manager.choose_profile` take the sprint's starter (the login on its `goal.stated` row, none for the owner's) and the logins that have a sprint waiting for a profile (an open sprint with a `waiting` manager row). After the pin, which outranks both, a colleague's account (the entry's `owner`) is dropped, from the room rule and from the most-recently-used fallback, for a sprint that is not his while one of his waits; among the rest, his own accounts with room answer for his sprint by the usual rule, and any other sprint chooses among every account with room. The owner's own accounts, which have no `owner`, are chosen as before, and the guest page and the owner's page show nothing new.
- Built in #1345: the colleague signs out or removes his own account from the guest door with `POST /api/guest-account-signout` and `/api/guest-account-remove`, each carrying `{"account": <number>}`, the number his pool row shows (the guest page never learns a profile name). The console selects the profiles of his that hold that number, by name, and the profile's ownership is checked again in the block that marks it as being changed, so a directory the owner replaced while the request waited is refused there and left untouched; an account that is another colleague's, the owner's or unknown is refused with a fixed text that names neither a login nor a profile. The owner's `POST /api/account-signout` signs a colleague's profile out and keeps it his, for a colleague's profile only. For a colleague's profile the sign-out or removal moves each manager on it as a stop followed by a takeover launch on the pool's choice (D5), refused as busy while a manager on it is reserved or was launched too recently to be listed, and a manager whose stop fails keeps the profile in place and answers `502`. A sign-out is recorded as a `signout` action row carrying the actor's login, and a removal row names who removed. The confirmation stays on the page, the guest write being the page's alone; excluding an account stays the owner's, and the guest door still refuses `/api/exclusion` even for his own profile. The owner's page offers Sign out beside a colleague's account that is not signed out, and its removal confirmation for a colleague's account says that its managers move to another account or wait for one with room, the exception to ADR-console-026 D6's "they are not stopped"; the owner's own account keeps that sentence. The guest page offers Sign out and Remove on his `yours` row only, Remove behind one confirmation that says its managers move on.
